How to Remove EXIF Data from Photos (iPhone, Android, Windows, Mac)

November 4, 2026Muhammad Abubakar

Your photos are talking behind your back. Here's how to shut them up — on every device you own.

Every photo you take with a phone or camera carries invisible baggage. It's called EXIF data — short for Exchangeable Image File Format — and it's embedded directly into the image file the moment the shutter fires.

We're not talking about something vague. We're talking about your exact GPS coordinates, accurate to a few meters. The date and time down to the second. Your device model. Your camera's lens information. Sometimes even a unique serial number that can link multiple photos back to the same device.

Nothing on the photo itself gives this away. No stamp, no watermark, no notification. It rides along silently, and anyone who gets the original file can extract it in seconds using any free metadata tool on the internet.

That selfie you're about to post? It might point straight to your apartment. That photo you're attaching to an email? Your home address could be baked into the file.

The fix is simple: remove the data before you share. Here's exactly how to do it on every major platform.

What EXIF Data Actually Contains

Before we get into the how, it helps to understand what you're dealing with. A typical photo from a modern smartphone stores all of this inside the file:

  • GPS coordinates — latitude, longitude, and sometimes altitude, pinpointing exactly where you were standing
  • Date and time — when the photo was taken, down to the second
  • Device model — "iPhone 15 Pro" or "Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra," for example
  • Camera settings — aperture, shutter speed, ISO, focal length
  • Software version — which operating system was running
  • Lens information — which camera module was used
  • Thumbnail — a small preview image that some tools forget to strip even when they remove everything else

The GPS data is the biggest privacy concern. But device information matters too — it can be used to fingerprint your photos and link them across platforms.

Remove EXIF Data on iPhone

Apple gives you a few built-in options, but they only go so far. Here's what works.

Option 1: Strip Location When Sharing (Built Into iOS)

This is the fastest method when you're about to send a photo and want the GPS coordinates removed from the shared copy.

  1. Open the photo in the Photos app.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Tap Options at the top of the share sheet.
  4. Turn off Location under the Include section.
  5. Tap Done and share normally.

The shared copy goes out without GPS data. Your original photo stays untouched in your library with its location intact.

What this doesn't do: It only strips location. The device model, timestamps, camera settings, and other EXIF fields all travel with the shared file. If you just need GPS gone, this is enough. If you want everything stripped, keep reading.

Option 2: Remove Location from the Photo Itself

If you want to permanently erase the GPS data from a photo in your library — not just from a shared copy — you can do that too.

  1. Open the photo in the Photos app.
  2. Tap the info button (the "i" icon) or swipe up on the photo.
  3. Tap Adjust next to the map/location field.
  4. Tap No Location.

The GPS data is now gone from the original file. This is permanent for that photo.

Option 3: Strip All Metadata (Third-Party App Needed)

iOS doesn't have a built-in way to remove all EXIF data — only location. To strip everything (device info, timestamps, camera settings, the lot), you need a third-party app.

  • Metapho — lets you view, edit, and remove all EXIF and XMP data. Handles single photos and batches.
  • ViewExif — lightweight viewer and editor for EXIF data.
  • Exif Eraser — single-purpose, strips everything.

Or skip the app install entirely and use a browser-based tool like our Remove GPS from Photo tool. It works directly in Safari on your iPhone — no download, no account.

Stop Your iPhone from Geotagging Future Photos

If you'd rather prevent the problem at the source:

  1. Go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services.
  2. Scroll down to Camera.
  3. Set it to Never.

New photos will no longer carry GPS data. Your existing library stays unchanged.

Remove EXIF Data on Android

Android's approach varies depending on your phone manufacturer, but the core methods are consistent.

Option 1: Remove Location via Google Photos

If you use Google Photos (which comes pre-installed on most Android devices):

  1. Open the photo in Google Photos.
  2. Swipe up or tap the three-dot menu to view details.
  3. Look for the location section and tap Remove location.

This removes GPS coordinates from the photo. However, this feature's behavior can be inconsistent across Android versions and manufacturer skins.

Important note: Google Photos only removes location from the copy in your local library. If the photo is backed up to Google's servers, Google may retain the original metadata on their end.

Option 2: Strip All Metadata (Third-Party App)

Just like iOS, Android doesn't offer a built-in "remove all EXIF" toggle. For a complete strip, grab one of these from the Play Store:

  • Photo Exif Editor — free, lets you view and selectively remove any metadata field.
  • Photo Metadata Remover — straightforward, batch-friendly, works offline.
  • ExifTool for Android — handles both photos and videos.

Or use a browser-based removal tool — it works the same way on your phone's browser as it does on desktop.

Stop Android from Tagging Future Photos

  1. Open your Camera app.
  2. Tap the gear icon (Settings).
  3. Look for Location tags, GPS tag, or Save location (the label varies by manufacturer).
  4. Turn it off.

Samsung labels it "Location tags." Pixel calls it "Save location." OnePlus uses "GPS tagging." The toggle is there — it just hides behind different names.

Remove EXIF Data on Windows

Windows has the most accessible built-in tool of any platform. No downloads needed.

Option 1: File Explorer (Built Into Windows)

  1. Open File Explorer and find the photo.
  2. Right-click the photo and select Properties.
  3. Click the Details tab.
  4. At the bottom, click Remove Properties and Personal Information.
  5. Choose your option:
    • Create a copy with all possible properties removed — makes a clean duplicate and leaves the original untouched.
    • Remove the following properties from this file — lets you pick specific fields to remove from the original.
  6. Click OK.

This handles GPS, camera information, timestamps, and most standard EXIF fields. It takes about five seconds per photo.

The limitation: Windows' built-in tool doesn't catch everything. Some XMP data, embedded thumbnails, and certain non-standard EXIF fields can survive the process. For casual sharing, it's more than adequate. For high-stakes privacy situations, use ExifTool or a dedicated online tool.

Remove EXIF Data on Mac

macOS doesn't have a single "strip all metadata" button built in, but you have a couple of paths.

Option 1: Preview Export (Quick, Mostly Effective)

  1. Open the image in Preview.
  2. Go to File → Export.
  3. Choose JPEG or PNG as the format.
  4. Click Save.

Exporting through Preview strips most EXIF data — GPS, camera model, timestamps. It preserves color profiles, which is actually what you want (stripping color profiles can shift how the image looks).

Option 2: ImageOptim (Free, Thorough)

ImageOptim is a free Mac app that strips metadata as part of its image optimization process. It's the most popular method for Mac users.

  1. Download ImageOptim from imageoptim.com.
  2. Open the app.
  3. Drag and drop your photos into the window.
  4. ImageOptim automatically removes all EXIF data and optimizes the file.

That's it. No settings to configure, no buttons to click. Drag in, metadata gone. It handles batch processing too — drag an entire folder in and it processes everything.

Option 3: Photos App (GPS Only)

If you just need to strip the location from a photo in your Apple Photos library:

  1. Open the photo in the Photos app.
  2. Press Cmd+I (Get Info).
  3. Click the location, then remove it.

Same limitation as iPhone — this only handles GPS data, not the rest of the EXIF package.

Remove EXIF Data Using the Command Line (All Platforms)

If you want total control — or need to process hundreds of photos at once — ExifTool by Phil Harvey is the standard. It's free, open source, and runs on Windows, Mac, and Linux.

Install ExifTool

  • Mac: brew install exiftool
  • Windows: Download from exiftool.org and add to your system path.
  • Linux: sudo apt install libimage-exiftool-perl

Common Commands

Strip all metadata from one photo:

exiftool -all= photo.jpg

Strip only GPS data (keep everything else):

exiftool -GPS:all= photo.jpg

Strip all metadata from every image in a folder:

exiftool -all= /path/to/folder/

Strip recursively through subfolders:

exiftool -r -all= /path/to/directory/

ExifTool is the benchmark. It removes every standard metadata tag — EXIF, IPTC, XMP, GPS, embedded thumbnails — from every major image format.

Which Platforms Strip EXIF Automatically?

Platforms that strip EXIF from the public copy: Instagram, Facebook, Twitter/X, Reddit, Discord, and TikTok all remove EXIF data from the version other people can download.

The catch: These platforms strip it from the public copy, but they keep your original metadata on their servers. They've already ingested your GPS coordinates, device information, and timestamps the moment you uploaded.

Platforms That Do NOT Strip EXIF

  • Email — attachments go through completely untouched.
  • Telegram — does not strip metadata by default.
  • iMessage — does not strip EXIF. The original file is delivered.
  • Cloud storage links — Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive serve the original file.
  • Forums and listing sites — most don't strip anything.
  • AirDrop — sends the original file with all metadata.

The rule of thumb: Never assume a platform strips your data. Strip it yourself before uploading.

How to Verify the EXIF Data Is Actually Gone

Removing metadata is only half the job. You should verify it worked.

Quickest method: Upload the cleaned photo to our Geo Tag Checker. If it comes back clean, you're good.

On Mac: Open the image in Preview → Tools → Show Inspector. Check the EXIF and GPS tabs.

On Windows: Right-click the image → Properties → Details. If the GPS and camera fields are blank, you're clear.

Command line (most thorough):

exiftool photo.jpg

When Should You Remove EXIF Data?

  • Before posting to any platform that doesn't auto-strip — forums, listing sites, blogs, email, messaging apps.
  • Before sending photos to people you don't fully trust — buyers, strangers, casual contacts.
  • Before uploading to your own website — especially if you're working from home or a private location.
  • Before sending client deliverables — your client doesn't need to know your home coordinates.
  • Before sharing photos of your kids — location data in family photos is a privacy risk that's easy to eliminate.

When it doesn't matter: sharing between family members who already know where you live, or uploading to platforms that are confirmed to strip metadata from the public copy.

Questions People Ask

Does removing EXIF data reduce photo quality?

No. EXIF data is stored in a separate section of the file from the image data itself. Removing it doesn't touch a single pixel. The photo looks identical — it's just lighter by a few kilobytes.

Can I remove EXIF from multiple photos at once?

Yes. On Mac, ImageOptim handles batches by drag and drop. On any platform, ExifTool processes entire folders with one command. On Windows, the built-in Properties tool lets you select multiple files at once.

Do screenshots contain EXIF data?

Minimal. Screenshots typically record the device model and timestamp but not GPS coordinates, since they're generated by the operating system rather than the camera. Still worth checking if you're cautious.

What about photos I've already shared?

If the photo was uploaded to a platform that strips EXIF publicly (Instagram, Facebook, etc.), the version other users downloaded is probably clean. But the version on the platform's servers still has your original data. There's no way to retroactively strip that. Going forward, strip before sharing.

Is there a way to remove EXIF from videos too?

Yes, though it's less straightforward. ExifTool supports video files. On iPhone, the share sheet Location toggle works for videos the same way it does for photos.
