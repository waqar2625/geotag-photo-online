Why You Might Need to Convert WebP to PNG

WebP is an excellent format for web delivery — it produces smaller files than both JPG and PNG. But its strength is also its weakness: it's a newer format that isn't universally supported by all software.

When you right-click and save an image from a modern website, you often get a .webp file. Many users find they can't open these files in their preferred image editor, can't upload them to certain platforms, or can't share them with people using older software. Converting to PNG solves all of these issues.

WebP vs PNG: The Tradeoff

WebP files are typically 26% smaller than PNGs at equivalent quality (for lossless) and significantly smaller for lossy compression. However, PNG has been around since 1996 and is supported by literally every image processing tool in existence. The conversion trades file size efficiency for universal compatibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is WebP?

WebP is an image format developed by Google that provides better compression than JPG and PNG. It supports both lossy and lossless compression, as well as transparency. It's widely used on websites for faster loading.

Why convert WebP to PNG?

Some image editors (older Photoshop versions, Paint.NET, some Linux viewers) don't support WebP. When you download an image from a website and get a .webp file, converting to PNG gives you a universally editable format.

Will the file size increase?

Yes, usually by 2-5x. PNG uses lossless compression which is less efficient than WebP. The tradeoff is universal compatibility and no further quality loss.

Is transparency preserved?

Yes. Both WebP and PNG support alpha transparency. If the WebP has transparent regions, they're preserved in the PNG output.

Can I convert animated WebP?

This tool converts the first frame of animated WebP images. For animated WebP to GIF conversion, a specialized tool is needed.
