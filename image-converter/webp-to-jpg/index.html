When to Convert WebP to JPG

WebP images are increasingly common on the web — Google Chrome, Firefox, and Edge all support them, and many websites serve WebP by default for better performance. But when you save these images and try to use them elsewhere, you often run into compatibility issues.

Printing Photos

Most photo printing services (CVS, Walgreens, Shutterfly, online print shops) accept JPG but not WebP. Convert to JPG before uploading to any print service.

Uploading to Platforms

Some social media platforms, job application portals, government forms, and e-commerce marketplaces don't accept WebP uploads. JPG is the safest universal choice.

Using in Older Software

Older versions of Photoshop, Microsoft Office, and many other applications don't support WebP. Converting to JPG lets you use the images in any application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why convert WebP to JPG?

WebP is a newer format that isn't supported by all software and platforms. Many image editors, printing services, and older applications need JPG. Converting ensures your images work everywhere.

What about transparency?

JPG doesn't support transparency. Any transparent areas in the WebP will be replaced with a white background. If you need transparency, convert to PNG instead.

Will quality decrease?

There can be slight quality differences since both formats use different compression algorithms. At 85-90% JPG quality, the visual difference from a lossy WebP is negligible.

Can I convert multiple files at once?

Yes, batch conversion is supported. Drop multiple .webp files and convert them all to JPG.
