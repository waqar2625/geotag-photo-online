Why Photo Metadata Is a Privacy Risk

Every photo taken with a smartphone contains hidden metadata that most people don't know about. This EXIF data includes your exact GPS location, device model, unique image ID, date and time down to the second, and sometimes even the direction you were facing.

When you share a photo online without removing this data, anyone who downloads it can extract these details. A photo taken at home reveals your home address. A photo taken at work reveals your workplace. A series of photos over time can map your daily routine.

What Information Is Hidden in Your Photos?

GPS Coordinates

The most sensitive field. If your phone had location services enabled (the default), every photo contains latitude and longitude accurate to within a few meters — enough to identify your exact street address.

Device Information

Your phone model, OS version, and sometimes hardware serial number are recorded. Combined with other photos, this can fingerprint your specific device across platforms.

Timestamps

Exact date and time including timezone. Combined with GPS, this creates a precise timeline of your movements.

Unique Identifiers

Some cameras embed unique image IDs (ImageUniqueID) or document IDs that can link photos back to a specific device across platforms and accounts.

When to Remove EXIF Data

Before Posting on Forums & Marketplaces

Platforms like Craigslist, Reddit, eBay, and WordPress don't strip EXIF. Photos posted there retain full metadata including GPS coordinates.

Before Sending Via Email

Email attachments preserve all EXIF data. Recipients get your GPS coordinates, device info, and timestamps.

Before Sharing Cloud Storage Links

Dropbox, Google Drive, and iCloud links give recipients the full file with all metadata. Strip EXIF before uploading if you'll share the link.

How Our EXIF Remover Works

When you upload a photo, the tool reads it into a Canvas element in your browser. The Canvas API renders only pixels — it doesn't reproduce EXIF metadata. The tool exports the Canvas as a new image file containing only pixels and format headers. All EXIF, IPTC, and XMP metadata is left behind.

For JPEG files, the EXIF orientation tag is applied to the Canvas before export, so visual orientation is preserved even after the orientation metadata is removed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What metadata does this tool remove?

It removes all EXIF metadata (camera info, GPS, date/time, exposure), IPTC metadata (keywords, captions, copyright), and XMP metadata (editing history, software tags). After stripping, only image pixels and essential format headers remain.

Why should I remove EXIF data before sharing photos?

Photos from smartphones contain GPS coordinates revealing your exact location. They also contain your device model, serial number, and unique image identifiers. This can be used to track movements, identify devices, and profile behavior.

Do social media platforms remove EXIF data?

Some do — Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, and Snapchat strip EXIF on upload. But many don't: Craigslist, WordPress, personal websites, most forums, email attachments, and cloud storage links (Google Drive, Dropbox) retain full metadata.

Does removing EXIF reduce image quality?

No. EXIF metadata is stored separately from the image data. Removing it doesn't recompress or modify the pixels. Visual quality is identical. File size drops slightly (5-50 KB) because the metadata bytes are gone.

Can I remove only GPS data and keep other EXIF?

This tool removes all metadata for maximum privacy. For selective removal, desktop software like ExifTool is needed. We recommend full removal before sharing — camera serial numbers and other fields can also be used for tracking.

Will removing EXIF affect photo orientation?

Some photos rely on the EXIF orientation tag to display correctly. Our tool handles this by physically rotating the image pixels to match the orientation before stripping the metadata.

Can removed EXIF data be recovered?

No. Once stripped and downloaded, the metadata is permanently gone from that copy. Your original file still has its metadata intact.

Does WhatsApp strip EXIF data?

Photos sent as 'Photos' (compressed) are stripped. Photos sent as 'Documents' (uncompressed) retain full EXIF data. Strip EXIF before sending as documents.
